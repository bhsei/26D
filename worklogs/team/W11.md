## Findings
- The design sequence diagrams of the five use cases share many classes (`PaymentMethods`, `ManagerApproval`, `TransactionRepository`, `StoreDatabaseSync`). The reviews found where two diagrams did the same step in different ways, for example the default tax rate, the cash refund after a manager approval and the void of a card payment with no answer; they now follow one pattern, with UC1 as the reference.
- Every new method of a diagram was sent to Borja, so the design class diagram stayed the single list of classes and methods; methods of the store database and of the repositories were also added to `system_interfaces.md`.
- The reviews found real gaps: a refund state saved outside the single database transaction, a sale that could be returned twice at two registers, a double scan of the same rental item, a customer registered a few minutes before at another register, and an HTTP GET that changed data.
- `detailed_design.md` was created on Mon 23 Nov, so some diagrams were pushed without their text first and the text was added the same day.
- Large diagrams again hit the size limit of the image and were split into several diagrams per use case.

## Decisions
- Question 5 is decided: the customer is identified only by the customer ID, which is printed on the rental receipt as text and as a barcode; no other personal data is stored (DR-18, NFR-12).
- A handler always gets the payment method with `PaymentMethods.get(tenderType)` and calls only the `PaymentMethod` interface, also for a cash refund after a manager approval and for the void of a card payment with no answer (AD-08).
- The first state of a card refund is saved in the same database transaction as the return (`saveCompleted`); `checkRefundStatus` is a POST, because it changes data.
- The quantities already returned of a sale are also asked to the store database; when it does not answer, the risk of a second refund at another register is accepted (open point 1 of `detailed_design.md` §7).
- Every architecture decision AD-01 to AD-10 is accepted.
